2. `docs/specs/2026-08-04-phase-9.1-identity-matching-remediation.md` §2 and §6 — the

   `backend/modules/reviews/find-product-page.ts` (`buildQueryVariants`,
   `findProductPageDetailed`, `runOpenQuery`) — this is where the money goes.

**The single most important constraint in this phase:** Phase 9.1's central lesson was
that *"the search failed"* and *"the search ran and found nothing"* are different facts,
and that collapsing them destroys good stored data. Every optimisation below is a way of
*not asking a question again*. Each one therefore sits exactly on top of that
distinction. A cost optimisation that caches a `request_failed` as if it were a
`zero_results` re-creates the 2026-08-05 data-loss defect with a longer fuse. Wherever
this spec says "remember a negative result," it means `zero_results` and only
`zero_results`.

## 1. Context in one paragraph

Serper credits are being consumed faster than expected. Measured against the current
code, a single wine enriched through both buttons costs roughly **42 credits**, and
about **90% of that is `POST /:id/fetch-reviews`**, not pricing. The driver is
structural rather than a bug: `fetchReviewData` loops every entry in `RETAILER_CONFIG`
and `findProductPageDetailed` issues up to four progressively-broader `site:`-scoped
queries per retailer, retrying whenever a variant returns zero organic results — which
is the *normal* outcome for the smaller shops. `RETAILER_CONFIG` grew from 4 entries
(`63160d4`, Phase 7) to 11 (`92c9d42`, Phase 7.3, 2026-07-29) to 12 (`9aafe88`, JJ
Buckley, 2026-08-02) without the per-wine query cost ever being revisited. It has not
changed since 2026-08-02; the cost is the 3× expansion that already happened, not a
recent one. Nothing auto-fetches — both routes are already behind explicit buttons — so
deferring work to a click is not available as a saving except in the one place noted in
WI-6.

## 2. Decisions already made — do not re-litigate

- **`RETAILER_CONFIG` is not shrinking.** Coverage stays; what changes is *when* each
  retailer is searched. Removing retailers would silently reintroduce the JJ Buckley
  failure mode documented in `docs/sessions/2026-08-02-review-sourcing-drift-analysis.md`
  (a retailer with real content producing a result indistinguishable from "we looked and
  found nothing").
- **K&L stays in `RETAILER_CONFIG`.** `modules/price/` depends on it for `klItemSeen`
  and `buildKlLinkOnlyResult`. Only the *review* search skips it.
- **Query-variant reduction is out of scope for this phase.** Cutting
  `buildQueryVariants` from four variants to two was evaluated and deliberately deferred:
  it is the only proposed change with a known recall cost (the Woodland Hills / Fèvre
  miss that motivated the relaxation in the first place). Revisit only if the budget is
  still over after this phase lands.
- **Vintage still ranks and never rejects** (Phase 9.1). No cost measure may turn a
  vintage gap into an early exit.
- **No new external dependency.** Counting and caching are local.

## 3. Cost model — measure this before and after

Per wine, both actions, current code:

| Path | Serper calls | Endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| `fetch-reviews` — configured loop | 12 retailers × 2–4 variants ≈ **36** | `/search` |
| `fetch-reviews` — discovered-merchant probe | ≤ 3 | `/search` |
| `fetch-reviews` — open-web fallback | ≤ 1 | `/search` |
| `fetch-price` — retailer/price discovery | **1** | `/shopping` |
| `fetch-price` — `resolveFallbackProductUrls` | ≤ 5, typically ~3 | `/search` |
| **Total** | **~42 typical, 25–57 range** | |

Two amplifiers on top of that: `fetchWithRetry` bills up to 3× on a 429, and
`SERPER_CONCURRENCY = 3` across 12 retailers is a burst large enough to *cause* the 429s
it then pays to retry (see the header comment in `shared/utils/concurrency.ts`, and the
2026-08-05 incident it documents). Nothing caches, so every "Refresh Reviews" click is a
full-price re-run.

**Measured position after this phase (WI-7, 2026-08-15).** Two separate measurements,
because they answer different questions:

*Leg 1a — untiered calibration probe.* `validate-reviews.ts`, extended with a
per-retailer yield table, run against all 14 wines with tiering deliberately bypassed
(every `RETAILER_CONFIG` entry probed directly) so every retailer's real yield could be
measured rather than assumed. **387 Serper credits for the 14-wine batch (~27.6/wine)**,
against a 250–500 pre-run estimate. Ranked on scores-per-credit (scores from a page
whose producer/denomination/bottling were accepted *and* whose vintage gap is exactly
0 — the only column that justifies primary tier, per §4):

| Retailer | Wines searched | zero_results | no_relevant_match | Pages found | Right-wine+vintage scores | Credits spent | Scores/credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jjbuckley | 14 | 9 | 1 | 4 | 8 | 29 | 0.276 |
| woodland | 14 | 6 | 2 | 6 | 7 | 31 | 0.226 |
| flatiron | 14 | 8 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 29 | 0.034 |
| winelibrary | 14 | 8 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 35 | 0.029 |
| kl | 14 | 3 | 0 | 11 | 0 | 20 | 0.000 |
| zachys | 14 | 11 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 36 | 0.000 |
| benchmark | 14 | 9 | 0 | 5 | 0 | 36 | 0.000 |
| sokolin | 14 | 11 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 35 | 0.000 |
| acker | 14 | 12 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 34 | 0.000 |
| morrell | 14 | 11 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 32 | 0.000 |
| crush | 14 | 11 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 33 | 0.000 |
| thatchers | 14 | 8 | 2 | 4 | 0 | 30 | 0.000 |

`benchmark` demoted primary → extended: zero right-wine-and-vintage scores across the
batch despite tying for the highest spend (36 credits — see `shared/config/retailers.config.ts`).
No promotions — nothing in the extended set scored above zero. Correctness cross-check
against `KNOWN_GOOD`/`KNOWN_BAD` in `ground-truth-wines.ts`: every trap still rejected
(Lafleur/Grand Village, Famille Perrin/Gour de Chaulé, Brumont/Montus, JJ Buckley
PDF/Clos Manou), no regressions found against the 2026-08-11 DB snapshot. One follow-up
filed separately (not fixed here, out of this phase's scope): a `bid.zachys.com/language?url=...`
auction-lot redirect shape slips past `isNonProductUrl`'s existing `bid.zachys.com`
patterns.

**Measured `p` = 7/14 = 0.5** — the fraction of wines where the reassigned primary tier
(`kl`, `woodland`, `jjbuckley`, `flatiron`, `winelibrary`) alone yields at least one
critic score, any vintage. Unchanged by benchmark's demotion: its one hit (Gour de
Chaulé) was already covered by another primary retailer that wine.

*Leg 1b — production tiered path.* Real server, `?force=true` on 3 of the 14 wines
(Bessin-Tremblay, Charles Audoin, Grand Village), `GET /api/debug/serper-usage`
before/after. **28 credits for reviews across 3 wines (~9.3/wine)** — primary tier
alone succeeded on all 3 (extended tier never fired; `avoided: 72` shows the 8 skipped
extended/unrenderable retailers' estimated cost). **3 credits for price (1/wine
exactly)**, confirming WI-6's fallback-URL removal. Re-hitting `fetch-reviews` without
`force` on the same wine returned `{cached: true, fetched_at}` with the usage counter
unchanged (31 → 31) — WI-4's near-zero re-fetch claim holds against the real route, not
just the offline suite.

The original formula-based table (`12 + 22(1 − p) + 1`, ~24 credits at `p=0.5`) is
superseded, not exactly matched: the real measured primary-tier cost per wine
(≈8.85 credits, from leg 1a's per-retailer spend) came in below the formula's assumed
12, because average variants-tried per retailer is lower in practice than the
worst-case ladder the formula assumed. First enrichment of a new wine now runs close
to **17–20 credits** (leg 1a's per-retailer averages) to **~20–24 credits** in the
worst case where the extended tier and discovered-merchant probe both fire, well
inside the original −43% target at `p=0.5`. Re-enrichment confirmed at **0 credits**
against a fresh TTL, exactly as designed.

## 4. Work items

### WI-1 — Instrument Serper spend

**Nothing else in this phase can be evaluated without this. Land it first, on its own
commit, and record a baseline before changing behaviour.**

Create `shared/utils/serper-client.ts` — `shared/` because both modules need it and
modules do not import each other (CLAUDE.md §5), the same reasoning that put
`concurrency.ts` there.

```ts
export type SerperEndpoint = 'search' | 'shopping'

export interface SerperCallRecord {
  endpoint: SerperEndpoint
  /** Attempts actually issued, including retries — this is what Serper bills. */
  attempts: number
  ok: boolean
  /** Free-text tag for attribution, e.g. 'reviews:configured:benchmark'. */
  label: string
}

/** Wraps fetchWithRetry, records the call, returns the Response unchanged. */
export async function serperFetch(
  endpoint: SerperEndpoint,
  body: unknown,
  apiKey: string,
  label: string
): Promise<Response>
```

- `fetchWithRetry` must report how many attempts it actually issued — extend it to
  return `{ res, attempts }` or accept an `onAttempt` callback. A retry is a billed
  request; counting one call per `serperFetch` under-reports exactly in the case that
  matters most.
- Migrate both production call sites: `runSerperQuery` in `reviews/find-product-page.ts`
  and the `fetch` in `price/serper-query.ts`. After this WI there must be **no direct
  `fetch` to `google.serper.dev` in `backend/modules/` or `shared/`** — add a grep-based
  unit test asserting that, so a future call site cannot silently escape accounting.
- There is a third, untracked call site: `backend/scripts/tmp-serper-health.ts` hits both
  endpoints directly. It is scratch (`tmp-` prefix, not referenced anywhere). Delete it,
  or migrate it and scope the grep test to include `backend/scripts/`. Either is fine —
  what is not fine is leaving an unaccounted spender in the repo while claiming the
  budget is measured.
- Accumulate per request via an `AsyncLocalStorage` context opened in the two route
  handlers, so one enrichment's calls are attributable to one wine without threading a
  counter through every function signature.
- On completion of `fetch-price` / `fetch-reviews`, log one structured line:
  `{ wine_id, action, calls, attempts, by_label }`.
- Add `GET /api/debug/serper-usage` returning process-lifetime totals grouped by label.
  Debug-only, no auth, not for the UI.

**Acceptance:** enrich one wine through both buttons; the logged `attempts` total matches
the delta on the Serper dashboard for that window, within rounding.

### WI-2 — Stop searching for reviews on domains that cannot be read

`UNRENDERABLE_DOMAINS` already exists in `find-product-page.ts` but is only consulted
inside `runOpenQuery`, i.e. the fallback passes. The *configured* loop in
`fetchReviewData` still spends a full variant ladder on `klwines.com` for every wine,
before a render that Phase 7 documented as permanently bot-blocked. The result is
guaranteed empty and has been since the feature shipped.

- Export `UNRENDERABLE_DOMAINS` from `find-product-page.ts` (it stays there — it is a
  property of the render step, which that module owns).
- In `fetchReviewData`, filter `RETAILER_CONFIG` before the `mapWithConcurrency` call:
  skip any retailer whose `domain` matches. Keep the full list for `attemptedDomains` —
  the fallback passes must still know K&L was never worth trying.
- Do **not** express this as a `reviewTier` value (see WI-3). Keep the two concepts
  apart: `UNRENDERABLE_DOMAINS` is a technical fact about whether a page can be read,
  `reviewTier` is a product judgement about coverage versus cost. `find-product-page.ts`
  already carries the comment *"This is a cost guard, not an access decision"* — preserve
  that separation in the opposite direction too. Conflating them would label a
  perfectly-readable low-yield shop "unrenderable," which is false and would mislead the
  next person to touch it.

**Acceptance:** a `fetch-reviews` run issues zero `/search` calls carrying
`site:klwines.com`. `price_data` still contains a K&L link-only entry whenever
`klItemSeen` is true — WI-2 must not change any price behaviour.

### WI-3 — Tier the review retailer list

Add to `RetailerConfig` in `shared/config/retailers.config.ts`:

```ts
/** Which review-sourcing pass this retailer is searched in (Phase 9.2).
 *  'primary'  — searched for every wine.
 *  'extended' — searched only when the primary pass yields no critic score.
 *  Cost, not trust: an extended retailer is fully trusted, just not paid for
 *  up front. See docs/specs/2026-08-12-phase-9.2-enrichment-cost-reduction.md. */
reviewTier: 'primary' | 'extended'
```

In `fetchReviewData`, replace the single `mapWithConcurrency(RETAILER_CONFIG, …)` with a
two-stage version. Extract the per-retailer body into a named local function first so
both stages call one implementation — this file has a documented history of the same
logic drifting between copies.

```
primary pass  → if any result has critic_scores.length > 0, stop here
extended pass → then the existing discovered-merchant probe, then open-web fallback
```

The existing gate is already the right shape: `!results.some(r => r.critic_scores.length > 0)`.
Reuse that exact predicate for the extended pass so all three escalations share one
definition of "found nothing."

**Seed assignment — explicitly provisional.**

| Tier | Retailers |
|---|---|
| `primary` | `kl`, `benchmark`, `woodland`, `jjbuckley`, `flatiron`, `winelibrary` |
| `extended` | `zachys`, `sokolin`, `acker`, `morrell`, `crush`, `thatchers` |

`kl` is listed primary on merit and is then skipped by WI-2's guard, which is the point
of keeping the two mechanisms separate. Effective primary pass is therefore 5 retailers.

This seeding is a starting guess drawn from which retailers produced *any* real product
page in the 2026-08-04 batch. **That evidence is contaminated** — it predates Phase 9.1,
and the batch's headline defect was scores attributed to the wrong wine entirely, so
"Benchmark yielded 5 scores" may mean Benchmark yielded 5 scores for a different wine.
Do not treat the table above as measured. WI-7 replaces it with real numbers.

Morrell deserves a specific note: it is `extended` here despite
`2026-08-02-review-sourcing-drift-analysis.md` demonstrating it *does* carry attributed
reviews (the Jean-Marc Vincent case). It failed for query-shape reasons that Phase 9.1's
honorific relaxation has since fixed, and it has never been measured post-fix. It is a
strong candidate to promote at WI-7.

**Acceptance:** a wine whose primary tier yields a score issues no `site:sokolin.com`
query. A wine whose primary tier yields nothing issues the full 12-retailer set minus
unrenderables, exactly as today. Ground-truth regression suite still green.

### WI-4 — Freshness guard on both enrichment routes

Today every click is a full-price re-run, and a double-click is two full-price re-runs.

- Both `POST /:id/fetch-price` and `POST /:id/fetch-reviews` accept `?force=true`.
- Without `force`, if the stored data's `fetched_at` is within its TTL, return the stored
  wine unchanged with `200` and a `{ cached: true, fetched_at }` marker. Issue no
  outbound calls.
- TTLs, as named constants with the reasoning in a comment: **price 7 days, reviews
  30 days.** Prices move; a critic score published against a vintage does not. These are
  different numbers because they are different kinds of fact, not because one matters
  more.
- **In-flight coalescing.** Keep a module-level `Map<wineId+action, Promise>`; a second
  request for the same wine and action while one is in flight awaits the first rather
  than starting its own. This is what actually stops double-click spend, and it is
  independent of the TTL.
- `force=true` bypasses the TTL *and* clears that wine's probe log (WI-5).

**UI (`WineCard.tsx`, `WineDetailModal.tsx`).** The buttons send no `force`. When a
response comes back `cached`, render "Reviews updated 3 days ago" with a secondary
"Refresh anyway" control that re-issues with `force=true`. One click to see what you
have; two clicks to spend money. Keep the existing button labels for the
never-fetched case.

### WI-5 — Remember which shops had nothing for this wine

A retailer that returned `zero_results` for a specific bottling burns the full variant
ladder every single run to learn the same thing. Whether Sokolin has ever indexed a page
for a given Chablis does not change week to week, even though prices do.

- Migration `backend/db/migrations/004_phase9_2_probe_log.sql`:
  `ALTER TABLE wines ADD COLUMN review_probe_log TEXT;` — JSON array of
  `{ slug, domain, stage, variants_tried, probed_at }`.
- Write one entry per retailer per `fetch-reviews` run, from the `Step1Outcome` that
  `findProductPageDetailed` already returns. Nothing new needs computing; the field
  exists and is currently used only by `validate-reviews.ts`.
- On a subsequent run, skip a retailer whose most recent entry is `stage: 'zero_results'`
  and is newer than `NEGATIVE_PROBE_TTL_DAYS` (start at **90**).
- **Skip on `zero_results` only.** Not `no_relevant_match` — results existed there, and
  Phase 9.1 changed the ranking that judges them, so it may now pick differently. And
  categorically never on `request_failed`: that is the exact conflation that erased eight
  wines' data on 2026-08-05. Assert this in a test that feeds a `request_failed` outcome
  and proves the next run re-queries.
- Log skips under a distinct `serperFetch` label so WI-1's report shows calls *avoided*,
  not just calls made.

### WI-6 — Resolve fallback product URLs on click, not on fetch

`resolveFallbackProductUrls` in `routes/wines.ts` spends up to 5 `/search` calls per
price fetch turning "a Google search for this shop" into "this shop's product page" — for
non-preferred shops, on links most users never open.

- Remove the call from the `fetch-price` handler. Fallback retailers keep the constructed
  Google search URL from `buildFallbackUrl`, which is what they had before Phase 9.1 and
  which always loads.
- Add `POST /api/wines/:id/resolve-retailer-url` taking `{ slug }`: runs
  `findMerchantProductPage` for that one merchant, and on success persists the resolved
  URL into the matching `price_data.retailers[]` entry with
  `is_search_results_page: false`. One credit, at the moment of intent, once per shop per
  wine forever.
- **Re-run `aggregatePriceData` after persisting.** `nearest_retailer` holds a *copy* of a
  retailer entry, so writing a URL into `retailers[]` without re-aggregating leaves the
  two disagreeing — the same class of stale-copy bug the current handler already guards
  against by re-aggregating after resolution.
- Frontend: a fallback retailer's "View" click calls this, then navigates to whatever
  comes back. Cap the wait at ~3s and navigate to the existing Google URL on timeout or
  failure. **The user must never be blocked by this** — it is an upgrade to the
  destination, not a precondition for leaving.

## 6. Out of scope — do not do these

- Reducing `buildQueryVariants` from four variants to two (see §2 — deferred, not
  rejected).
- Removing any retailer from `RETAILER_CONFIG`.
- Changing `TARGET_RETAILER_COUNT` in `serper-query.ts`. It does not affect the

  never searched is never rendered.

- Any auto-enrichment or background refresh. Enrichment stays user-initiated.

## 7. Definition of done

- `GET /api/debug/serper-usage` reports per-label call and attempt counts, and no direct
  `fetch` to `google.serper.dev` exists outside `serper-client.ts` (enforced by test).
- A wine enriched twice in a row spends its full cost once and ~0 the second time.
- A `fetch-reviews` run issues no query against a domain in `UNRENDERABLE_DOMAINS`.
- A wine whose primary tier yields a critic score never queries an extended retailer.
- A `request_failed` probe is provably re-queried on the next run; a `zero_results` probe
  provably is not, until the TTL expires.
- `fetch-price` issues exactly one Serper call.
- Clicking a fallback retailer's "View" reaches its product page when resolvable, and its
  Google search otherwise, without blocking longer than the timeout.
- Existing suites green: 318 backend tests / 40 web tests as of the Phase 9.1 hand-off,
  plus new tests for the TTL guard, the `request_failed` re-query, and the tier
  escalation. `tsc --noEmit` clean in both projects.
- §3's expected-position table is replaced with measured before/after figures from WI-7.
