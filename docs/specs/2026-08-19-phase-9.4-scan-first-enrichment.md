## 1. The problem in one paragraph

Phase 9.3 put reviews at the top of the post-save screen but left them click-gated, so
the sequence the developer actually experiences is: scan, confirm fields, save, land on
the review screen, *then* click Fetch Reviews and wait. The review data — the signal that
decides whether the wine is worth keeping at all — arrives after the wine is already in
the collection, which means the screen cannot do the job its name claims. Two things have
to change together. The fetch moves earlier, firing the moment GPT-4o label parsing
returns, so the seconds spent verifying the parsed fields double as the seconds the search
needs. And the *commitment* moves later: a scan no longer lands in the Discovered list by
default, because the whole point of showing reviews first is to let the developer decide,
and a decision that has already been made for you is not a decision. The wine is written
to the database immediately — it needs an id for enrichment to attach to — but it does not
become part of the collection until the developer says so, with at least one list tag
chosen.

## 2. Decisions made 2026-08-19 — do not re-litigate

- **A scanned wine is a draft until affirmed.** It is persisted immediately (enrichment
  needs an id) but appears in no list, no count, and no query until the developer clicks
  **Save to Collection** with at least one list tag selected.
- **`tag_discovered` is no longer applied by default.** It becomes one of three
  explicit choices on the review screen — Discovered, Wishlist, Cellar — and at least one
  must be picked before Save to Collection is enabled. This ends the "every wine starts in
  Discovered" rule that has held since Phase 5.
- **Draft state is a `promoted_at` timestamp, not a tag.** Rationale in WI-2 — the short
  version is that tags are a list model and draft-ness is a lifecycle fact, and collapsing
  them makes an abandoned scan indistinguishable from a wine deliberately untagged later,
  which in turn makes automatic cleanup impossible.
- **Drafts are swept after 24 hours.** Long enough to resume an interrupted session, short
  enough that they never become a performance or clutter problem.
- **Auto-fire is bounded to the `primary` review tier.** Not the extended tier, the
  discovered-merchant probes, or the open-web fallback. The binding reason is **latency,
  not credits** (see §3 — credits turn out to have plenty of headroom): those three stages
  are three more sequential Serper→Puppeteer→GPT-4o rounds and will not finish in the time
  it takes to check four fields, so auto-firing them buys a wait, not a result.
- **Escalation stays user-initiated**, as a button on the Discovery Review screen shown
  when the primary tier returned no critic score.
- **A scan that matches a wine already in the collection spends nothing.** Client-side
  `scoreMatch` against the already-loaded wine list, before any row is created or any call
  is fired.
- **This is a scoped reversal of "enrichment stays user-initiated," not a repeal.**
  Auto-fire applies to: the primary review tier, on the scan creation path, once per wine,
  after a free duplicate check. Anywhere else, the rule stands.

## 3. Cost arithmetic — measured, and against the actual billing state

**Billing state as of 2026-08-19 (developer-confirmed):** the 2,500-credit free
allowance is **exhausted**. The project is on its **first $50 Starter pack — 50,000
credits, expiring six months from purchase** (developer-confirmed 2026-08-19). (The
earlier note in
`wine-app-product-context.md` §6 describing the free tier as "2,500 queries/month" was
wrong on two counts — it was one-time, and it is gone. WI-8 corrects it.)

Per-stage costs, recovered from `validate-reviews-results.json` (the 14-wine untiered
batch, 2026-08-15, 387 credits) by summing `variantsTried` per retailer and splitting on
`reviewTier`:

| Stage | Credits per wine (measured avg) | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Price — one Shopping call | 1 | 1 |
| Reviews, `primary` tier (4 renderable; K&L skipped as unrenderable) | **8.9** | 6–12 |
| Reviews, escalation (`extended` 16.9 + ≤3 merchant probes + 1 open-web) | **~21** | 15–25 |

Primary tier alone yielded at least one critic score for **7 of 14** wines — the same
`p = 0.5` already recorded in `retailers.config.ts`.

| Flow | Credits per scan | Six-month burn at 20–50 scans/mo | % of the 50,000-credit pack |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today (Phase 9.3): price auto, reviews on click | 1 + (0 or ~20) | ~1,300–5,000 | 3–10% |
| Original proposal: full ladder auto-fired | ~21 avg, ~31 worst | ~2,500–6,300 | **5–13%** |
| This phase: price + primary auto, escalation clicked | **~10 auto**, +21 escalated | ~2,000–5,100 | **4–10%** |

**Read that table honestly: Serper credits are not the binding constraint, and the
original proposal was affordable.** At the developer's stated volume even the most
expensive option consumes an eighth of the pack over six months — roughly **five years of
runway** at the current scan rate, and it would take about **10× that rate** for the
choice between these three flows to have any dollar consequence at all.

The leftover is forfeited: credits expire at six months, so ~90% of the pack will go
unspent and the effective cost is a flat **$8.33/month** — above the stated $5/month target
by pack shape rather than by usage. Nothing in this application can change that; only a
provider with smaller packs or without expiry could. **The design conclusion follows:** the
money is already spent and the unused remainder is lost either way, so a credit saved on
an individual scan is not a dollar saved.

So the primary-tier decision in §2 should be understood as a **latency and correctness**
decision that happens to also be cheaper, not as a cost saving. Two costs *are* genuinely
per-scan and unbounded by the pack, and both argue the same way:

- **GPT-4o extraction.** Auto-firing makes it unconditional: the primary tier renders and
  extracts up to 4 pages per wine, plus the ~$0.004 label scan. Order of a few cents per
  scan, ~$0.50–$2/month at this volume. The full ladder would roughly triple it.
- **The developer's time.** Escalation is three sequential Serper→Puppeteer→GPT-4o stages.
  Auto-firing them means half of all scans sit on a spinner past the point where the
  verification work is done.

Because WI-3 makes the tier a request parameter, widening auto-fire later is a one-line
change if the developer decides the latency is tolerable after living with it. Nothing
here is a one-way door.

## 4. Work items

### WI-1 — Create the wine row on parse, not on confirm

`fetch-reviews` and `fetch-price` are both `POST /api/wines/:id/...` and write to a row.
Firing them "as soon as parsing completes" therefore requires an id at that moment. Two
ways to get one; take the first.

- **Take:** move `createWine` from `LabelScanFlow`'s `handleConfirm` to immediately after
  a successful `scanLabel`. `FlowState`'s `review` step carries the created `WineEntry`
  instead of a bare `LabelScanResult`. The edit screen becomes a PATCH form over a real
  row (`updateWine`) rather than a builder for a `CreateWineInput`.
- **Reject:** a new id-less "preview enrichment" endpoint keyed on identity. That is a
  second call path into the same two modules, with its own cache, freshness and
  accounting — exactly the duplication Phase 9.3 removed when it deleted `EnrichingCard`'s
  bespoke price `useEffect`.

**Acceptance:** `scanLabel` resolving is immediately followed by a created row with an
`id`. The edit screen mutates that row. No `CreateWineInput` is built by the scan path.

### WI-2 — Draft and promotion model

This is the schema-touching work item; read it before WI-1's code lands, because WI-1
without it puts untriaged rows in the Discovered list.

**Representation — `promoted_at`, migration `005_phase9_4_draft_promotion.sql`:**

```sql
ALTER TABLE wines ADD COLUMN promoted_at TEXT;          -- ISO timestamp; NULL = draft
UPDATE wines SET promoted_at = date_added WHERE promoted_at IS NULL;  -- backfill: everything
                                                        -- existing is already in a list
CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS idx_wines_promoted_at ON wines(promoted_at);
ALTER TABLE wines ...                                    -- see tag_discovered note below
```

`tag_discovered`'s column default changes from `1` to `0`. Existing rows are untouched by
that change (SQLite defaults apply to inserts only); the backfill above is what keeps the
current collection visible.

Why a column and not `tag_discovered = 0`: the developer's stated priorities are app
performance and not being cluttered by wines they don't care about. A boolean-tag encoding
satisfies neither at the point that matters. An abandoned draft with all tags false is
byte-identical to a wine the developer deliberately removed from every list — which the
tag model explicitly permits (`wine-app-product-context.md` §3, "User removes it manually")
— so no sweeper can ever distinguish them, drafts accumulate permanently, and every list
query scans a table that grows with abandoned scans rather than with the collection. A
nullable timestamp costs one column, makes "is this in the collection" a single indexed
predicate, and makes WI-7's sweep expressible as one bounded query. It also keeps tags
meaning exactly one thing — which lists a wine appears in — rather than two.

**Promotion:**

- `POST /api/wines/:id/promote`, body `{ tag_discovered?, tag_wishlist?, tag_cellar? }`.
  Rejects with 400 when all three are false or absent — **at least one list tag is
  required**. Sets the supplied tags and `promoted_at = now()` in one transaction.
- Not a `PATCH` special case: promotion is a state transition with a precondition, and
  burying it in the general update route means every future caller has to remember the
  precondition. The route is free (no metered call), so this costs nothing but a handler.
- Promotion is idempotent — re-promoting an already-promoted wine updates tags and leaves
  `promoted_at` at its original value.

**Reads:**

- `WineFilter` gains `include_drafts?: boolean`, defaulting **false**. `listWines` filters
  `promoted_at IS NOT NULL` unless it is set. Every existing caller is therefore correct
  without modification, which is the point of defaulting this way.
- `getWine(id)` returns drafts — the review screen needs to read the row it is enriching.
- Counts, tab badges and the tag-filtered list views inherit the `listWines` default and
  need no changes.

**Acceptance:** a scanned-but-unaffirmed wine appears in no list view and no count. Save
to Collection is disabled until at least one tag is selected. `POST /promote` with no tags
returns 400.

### WI-3 — `tier` parameter on `POST /:id/fetch-reviews`

- Accept `?tier=primary|full`, defaulting to **`full`** so `WineCard` and
  `WineDetailModal`'s existing buttons are unchanged.
- Thread it into `fetchReviewData` as a `ReviewFetchOptions` field. `tier: 'primary'` runs
  the primary pass and returns — it does not evaluate `foundNoScore()` for the extended
  pass, the discovered-merchant pass, or the open-web fallback.
- Record the three skipped stages via `recordAvoidedCalls` with a distinct label
  (`reviews:skipped:tier-primary:*`) so the usage report can tell "cheap because tiered"
  apart from "cheap because there was nothing to search."

This adds a parameter to an existing route. **No new Serper call site** — the constraint
Phase 9.3 §6 and CLAUDE.md §5 actually set.

**Acceptance:** `tier=primary` issues Serper calls only for `reviewTier === 'primary'`
retailers, verified by a test counting labelled calls through `serper-client`.

### WI-4 — Free duplicate check before any spend

- On parse, run `scoreMatch` (`shared/utils/wine-match.ts`) against the wine list already
  in `App.tsx` state. No new endpoint, no new query, no network call.
- **Match against promoted wines only.** The list in state already excludes drafts (WI-2),
  so this is free — but it must stay that way deliberately: short-circuiting to a
  half-finished draft would show the developer a screen mid-enrichment and hide the wine
  they were actually scanning.
- On a confident match (producer + denomination match, vintage match), skip WI-1's
  creation entirely and route straight to the existing entry's Discovery Review screen,
  showing its stored `review_data`/`price_data` with `EnrichmentFreshness` ages. That
  screen shows the wine's current tags rather than a Save to Collection button, since it
  is already promoted.
- Offer "This is a different bottle — add anyway," which falls through to the normal path.
  A vintage mismatch against an otherwise-matching wine should present as a distinct case
  ("You have the 2021 — this is the 2022"), not as a silent non-match.

**Acceptance:** re-scanning a bottle already in the collection issues zero Serper calls and
zero OpenAI calls beyond the label scan itself. Covered by a test asserting no enrichment
fetcher is invoked.

### WI-5 — Make the reviews TTL tier-aware

A primary-tier run stores `review_data` with a fresh `fetched_at`. A later `tier=full`
click would then hit `isWithinTtl(..., REVIEWS_TTL_DAYS)` and be served the cached
primary-only result — the escalation button would silently do nothing for 30 days.

- `force=true` is **not** the fix. Forcing also clears the wine's probe log, discarding the
  negative-probe memory that keeps a later full run cheap.
- Instead: a `tier=full` request is satisfied by cache only if the stored data was itself
  produced by a full run. Infer this from the probe log rather than adding a column — if
  `review_probe_log` holds no entry for any `reviewTier === 'extended'` retailer, the
  stored result is primary-only and a `full` request must proceed.
- `tier=primary` requests keep the existing TTL behaviour unchanged.

**Acceptance:** auto-fire (primary) → click escalate (full) actually escalates. Click
escalate twice → the second is served from cache. Both covered in
`tests/unit/enrichment-cache.test.ts`.

### WI-6 — Wire the flow in `LabelScanFlow` / `DiscoveryReview`

Sequence, after WI-1, WI-2 and WI-4:

1. `scanLabel` returns → duplicate check (WI-4) → draft row created (WI-1, WI-2).
2. Fire `fetchWinePrice` and `fetchWineReviews(id, { tier: 'primary' })` **in parallel**.
   They can be parallel specifically *because* the auto path is primary-only: the
   discovered-merchant pass is the only stage that consumes `price_data.retailers`, and it
   does not run at this tier. **If auto-fire is ever widened past primary, this must become
   sequential** — leave that as a code comment, not just a spec line.
3. Render the edit/verify screen immediately. Do not block it on either fetch.
4. **Hold the fetch when Tier 1 is incomplete.** If `producer`, `denomination` or `vintage`
   came back null from the scan, skip step 2 and fire on Continue instead — a search
   missing the producer is near-guaranteed to spend ~9 credits and a handful of GPT-4o
   calls on nothing.
5. **Re-fire on identity edit.** On Continue, compare `producer`, `denomination`,
   `vintage`, `cuvee`, `vineyard` against their parsed values. If any changed, abort the
   in-flight run and re-fire (the first run's spend is sunk, and bounded at ~10). If none
   changed, keep the in-flight result. Non-identity edits (region, quality classification)
   never re-fire.
6. Continue → `DiscoveryReview`, showing results already arriving or arrived, with
   `EnrichmentFreshness` as today.

`DiscoveryReview` changes:

- The header stops claiming "✓ Saved to Collection" for a draft — it has not been. Use a
  neutral state for `promoted_at === null`.
- Fetch Reviews becomes **"Search more retailers"**, shown only when
  `criticScores.length === 0`, calling `reviews.run({ tier: 'full' })`. Its copy should say
  what it does in plain terms ("searches 7 more shops"). When scores are already present,
  the existing Refresh Reviews behaviour (full tier, force on second click) stands.
- The tag row becomes the decision control: Discovered / Wishlist / Cellar, multi-select,
  none selected initially.
- **Save to Collection** — disabled until at least one tag is selected; calls
  `POST /:id/promote`; then closes and refreshes the list.
- **Discard** — calls `DELETE` (WI-7); closes without adding anything.
- Closing the screen any other way (escape, overlay click, browser close) leaves the draft
  for the sweep. It does not silently promote.

**Acceptance:** review data is visible on the Discovery Review screen without a click for
any wine whose primary tier found a score. The escalation button appears only when it did
not. Nothing reaches any list without Save to Collection.

### WI-7 — Discard, and the 24-hour draft sweep

- `DELETE /api/wines/:id` in `backend/routes/wines.ts` (currently `POST`, `GET`, `PATCH`
  only) plus the adapter method. Deletes the wine's `review_probe_log` rows with it.
- Refuses (409) when the wine has tasting notes — never cascade a note away silently.
- Discard on both the edit screen (Cancel) and `DiscoveryReview` calls it.
- **Sweep:** on server start and once per 24h thereafter, delete drafts where
  `promoted_at IS NULL AND date_added < now - 24h`. One bounded, indexed query. Log the
  count deleted; do not report it in the UI.
- Trade-off, stated so it is a decision and not an accident: a swept draft takes its
  `review_data` with it, so re-scanning that wine more than 24 hours later costs ~10
  credits again. Given §3's headroom that is the right trade — the alternative is
  unbounded invisible rows, which is the clutter and performance cost the developer asked
  to avoid.

**Acceptance:** cancelling a scan leaves no row. A wine with a tasting note cannot be
deleted. A draft older than 24h is gone after the next sweep; a promoted wine of any age
never is.

## 6. Out of scope — do not do these

- Auto-firing the extended tier, merchant probes, or open-web fallback. Cost is no longer
  the argument (§3); latency and GPT-4o spend are. Behind a click.
- Auto-fire on the manual `+ Add Wine` path. A hand-typed wine has no parse to verify and
  no waiting time to fill. It **does** get the draft/promote treatment (WI-2 applies to
  every creation path) — it just does not get the automatic review fetch.
- Any new Serper call site or query variant. WI-3 adds a parameter to an existing route;
  net new call sites: **zero**.
- Background or scheduled enrichment refresh. Still ruled out — the sweep in WI-7 is a
  deletion job and touches no metered API.
- Reviving `cellar_category`, or persisting the label image. Both still open questions
  (`wine-app-product-context.md` §8), neither is this phase.
- Evaluating alternative SERP providers. §3's pack-shape finding makes it worth an open
  question, not a work item here.
- iOS. Web only.

## 7. Definition of done

- A scanned wine appears in no list, count or tab until Save to Collection is pressed with
  at least one list tag selected.
- `tag_discovered` is never set by any automatic path.
- A scan of a wine not already in the collection shows critic scores on the Discovery
  Review screen without a click, whenever the primary tier finds one (measured: ~half).
- A scan of a wine already in the collection issues zero metered calls.
- A scan whose parse is missing producer, denomination or vintage fires nothing until
  Continue. Editing an identity field before Continue re-fires; editing a non-identity
  field does not.
- The escalation button appears only when the primary tier found no score, and actually
  escalates (is not swallowed by the 30-day TTL).
- Cancelling a scan leaves no row; an abandoned draft is gone within 24 hours; a wine with
  a tasting note cannot be deleted.
- `GET /api/debug/serper-usage` shows ~10 attempts for a non-escalated scan, attributed to
  that wine.
- Docs reflect the real Serper billing state and the end of automatic `tag_discovered`.
- Existing backend/web suites green; `tsc --noEmit` clean in both projects.
